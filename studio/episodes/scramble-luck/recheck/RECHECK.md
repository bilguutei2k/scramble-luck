## Interpretations fixed before comparison

- Exclusions are sequential, at the solver-result level, starting with all raw
  results in 2019–2025 confirmation competitions: other event, nonfinal, invalid
  format, schedule, attempt-slot structure, status. Schedule reasons also count
  distinct affected finals. An absent schedule is separate from a malformed one.
  One non-extra group, exactly five nonempty regular rows numbered 1–5 are needed;
  extra pools do not exclude a panel. Invalid status quarantines the solver row.
- Solver-seasons count distinct eligible competitions; multiple final rounds for
  one solver in one competition would require clarification. The actual included
  data are checked for duplicate solver/final rows. Under-two-success rows stay
  in exposure and extrema; their contrast matrix is zero.
- Skill reference uses other eligible finals with at least one successful solve,
  takes each final's mean successful log time, then its median over at least two
  distinct other competitions within ±2 years. Missing-reference and uninformative
  rows do not enter either tier. Median ties belong to the slow tier. Both tiers
  must have at least three informative reference-bearing solvers.
- The instruction to fit pooled positions separately by tier is implemented as
  beta_t = pinv(sum C_i) sum C_i y_i on the tier-supported sample. This is
  algebraically identical to first subtracting frozen beta and fitting a free
  pooled residual position effect. Per-tier S, V and cross covariance use those
  fitted residuals. Moments remain signed. No complete-case or speed filter.
- Era F is refitted on the era's tier panels while skill references retain the
  registered full 2019–2025 ±2-year reference window. Era R uses the same eligible
  person-year roster and frozen beta, restricted by year; a season cannot cross
  the era boundary.
- Exact R point estimates use the five registered beta numbers, without refitting
  them on confirmation. Ties use raw centiseconds, competition ID, round ID,
  attempt number, result ID. All weighted copies of the focal solver are removed
  from the peer matrix. Eigenvalues strictly greater than 1e-9 determine rank.
- The bootstrap competition universe is the distinct included confirmation
  competitions; the solver universe is the union of included confirmation and
  pre-2019 exploration solvers, so the same weight applies on both sides. Training
  competitions have a separate independent multinomial. Draw order is confirmation
  competition, solver, training competition, using NumPy default_rng(20261001),
  lexicographic cluster ordering and 2,000 draws. The registration does not name
  a random-number generator or uniquely specify the solver universe.
- Within each resampled season, competition multiplicity changes availability
  but does not duplicate the minimum: a solver with multiplicity v contributes
  v copies of its season. Thus reselecting R has solver weights, not the winning
  competition's multiplicity. This follows equal-person-year aggregation. The
  fixed-selection sensitivity explicitly uses competition × solver multiplicity
  as Amendment 1 requires. Peer estimates are invariant to the common positive
  competition weight within a final, so it cancels exactly.
- Bootstrap training beta is fitted by the pooled successful-slot centering
  equations with product weights. Training variance moments do not enter the
  unshrunk R endpoint. Recomputing references/F per draw is unnecessary for the
  requested R interval; this recheck computes F and gamma/D points, not intervals.
- Absent-season diagnostics count original roster entries with no represented
  solver/successful selected row after resampling. They include solver dropout;
  rank failures are separate. The original five-final roster is never reapplied.
- The amended interval uses linear quantiles of log R minus its bootstrap mean,
  added to the original log R and exponentiated. Its verdict is conditional on
  the owner's approved validation amendment; this task does not rerun the gate,
  optimal-cross mechanism study, DNF regression, or other unrequested endpoints.

### Bootstrap comparison and Monte Carlo tolerance

Both implementations use the same cluster universes, order, seed, training
refits, seasonal reselection, focal exclusion, solver weighting of selected
seasons, and 2,000 valid draws. My code calls `rng.multinomial`; Sol's
`analysis/engine.py:165`–169 calls `bincount(rng.integers(...))`. These produce
exactly the same multinomial law but consume the seeded random stream
differently. Numerical equality of the finite draw collections is not expected.
The R amended interval, raw interval and fixed-selection interval all agree
within Monte Carlo error, as do the bootstrap mean/displacement and exposure
means. My amended interval is [1.0163694284, 1.0231592116]; Sol's is
[1.0163698055, 1.0230705471]. Both decisively give **Belief fails** under the
accepted amended contract; this is not a discrepancy in interval centering.

`code/diagnose.py` estimates Monte Carlo SE by 1,000 independent resamples of
my 2,000 saved draws (diagnostic seed 20261002). Centered endpoint SEs on the
log scale are 0.00012259 and 0.00009669. Each endpoint tolerance is four times
the combined SE of two equal-sized independent draw collections, i.e.
4*sqrt(2)*SE: approximately 0.0006935 and 0.0005469 log units. Mean diagnostics
and raw/fixed endpoints use their separately estimated SEs. These tolerances
measure finite-bootstrap simulation error, not scientific sampling uncertainty;
they are never applied to observed counts or deterministic point estimates.
The actual amended endpoint log differences are below 0.000087. Full tolerances
and all comparisons are in `analysis/recheck_report.json`.
