### Source and date conventions

The intended authority is the [WCA 3×3 world-record history page](https://www.worldcubeassociation.org/results/records?event_id=333&show=history). It returned access errors during this research, including HTTP 403. Accordingly, the tables link accessible official competition announcements/highlights, Guinness, and cubing reporting; the [SpeedSolving history index](https://www.speedsolving.com/wiki/index.php/List_of_World_Records/3x3x3) is a supplementary cross-check, not a substitute for a fresh WCA records-page check before publication. A range explicitly means **competition dates**, not a verified day of the record round. WCA dates results to the last calendar date of their round and recognizes only the best same-day record result. Story inference: **a shortcut in one solution is not a measurement of an advantage shared by the field**. ## 3. How finals work, and the slot confound

The linked historical edition matters because the study covers 2019–2025; the current rules are dated April 1, 2026. Do not project the new Head to Head procedures back onto ordinary average-of-five finals. [Current regulations](https://www.worldcubeassociation.org/regulations/), [July 2025 edition](https://www.worldcubeassociation.org/regulations/history/official/2025-07-17/)

- **Randomness and secrecy.** Regulation 4b requires an official scramble program; 4b1 prohibits inspecting/filtering generated sequences. For 3×3, 4b3 specifies equal-probability random states requiring at least two moves. Regulation 4g requires scramble verification. This is random-state generation, not a guarantee of equal human difficulty. [4b](https://www.worldcubeassociation.org/regulations/#4b), [4b1](https://www.worldcubeassociation.org/regulations/#4b1), [4b3](https://www.worldcubeassociation.org/regulations/#4b3), [4g](https://www.worldcubeassociation.org/regulations/#4g)
- **Groups.** Regulation 1h permits multiple groups; 1h+ recommends common sequences within a group and different sequences across groups; 1h++ recommends one shared set in finals. These say **should**, not **must**. The episode explicitly restricts itself to one-group finals. [1h and recommendations](https://www.worldcubeassociation.org/regulations/#1h), [episode scope](../EPISODE.md#scope)
- **Extra/replacement attempts.** The Delegate may grant an extra for an incident under 11e. Under **11e1**, it must use a different officially generated sequence; under 11e2 it replaces the original numbered attempt. Results completed out of order retain scramble-sequence order under 11e2a. [11e](https://www.worldcubeassociation.org/regulations/#11e), [11e1](https://www.worldcubeassociation.org/regulations/#11e1), [11e2–11e2a](https://www.worldcubeassociation.org/regulations/#11e2)
- **Average of five.** Five attempts; discard fastest and slowest; mean the middle three. One DNF/DNS can be discarded; two make the average DNF. A spectacular single is therefore discarded from its own average. [2025 rules 9f8–9f9](https://www.worldcubeassociation.org/regulations/history/official/2025-07-17/#9f8)

**Competitor order is not scramble order.** Article A describes individual readiness, uncovering, inspection and timer starts. It permits a waiting area or competitors staying at assigned stations. It does not prescribe a universal seed order or a common start signal for an ordinary final. Thus several competitors can be solving concurrently without starting simultaneously; organizers can also call competitors successively. This is an inference from the procedures, not evidence of the actual arrangement at every sampled competition. [2025 A2a1–A2a2](https://www.worldcubeassociation.org/regulations/history/official/2025-07-17/#A2a1), [2025 A3b1–A3b2](https://www.worldcubeassociation.org/regulations/history/official/2025-07-17/#A3b1)

Avoid “all finalists race the same cube at the same moment” and any universal “slowest seed first” claim. Use: **“In the one-group finals we're studying, competitors are assigned the same five scheduled scrambles.”** The word scheduled leaves room for replacements; the study's eligibility and limits come from [EPISODE.md](../EPISODE.md) and [METHOD.md](../METHOD.md). For contrast only, the 2026 Head to Head rules explicitly specify an announcer's common inspection start; those procedures are separate. [I4c](https://www.worldcubeassociation.org/regulations/#I4c)

**Implication for the slot confound — methodological inference:** common attempt positions may overlap in time, or occupy successive waves. Noise, interruptions, changing conditions, or attempt-position effects could then track the scheduled scramble. Conversely, staggered solves need not share a precise instant of exposure. The rules alone cannot establish wall-clock synchronization or how often these effects occur. Consequently label the estimate **shared scramble/slot component**, and retain the method's explicit limitation: it cannot isolate scramble difficulty from conditions shared by its time slot. A replacement can also occupy the original result position while using a different sequence. [METHOD.md, lines 1–60](../METHOD.md), [11e1 and 11e2a](https://www.worldcubeassociation.org/regulations/#11e1)

## 4. Cubing basics — two lines each

**CFOP:** A common speed-solving method: Cross, First Two Layers, Orient Last Layer, Permute Last Layer. [CubeSkills](https://www.cubeskills.com/tools/glossary), [CFOP guide](https://ruwix.com/the-rubiks-cube/advanced-cfop-fridrich/)  
Build the base, fill the first two layers with corner-edge pairs, then orient and place the remaining pieces. [CFOP guide](https://ruwix.com/the-rubiks-cube/advanced-cfop-fridrich/)

**Cross:** Four edge pieces form a plus around one face's center, with their side colors matching adjacent centers. [Cross section of the CFOP guide](https://ruwix.com/the-rubiks-cube/advanced-cfop-fridrich/)  
An X-cross solves a corner-edge pair alongside that cross; double/triple X-crosses extend that idea to two/three pairs. [CubeSkills](https://www.cubeskills.com/tools/glossary), [Park reconstruction](https://reco.nz/solve/9155), [Zajder reconstruction](https://reco.nz/solve/12564)

**PLL skip:** After orienting the last layer, its pieces already occupy the right relative positions, so the permutation algorithm is unnecessary. [PLL guide](https://ruwix.com/the-rubiks-cube/advanced-cfop-fridrich/permutate-the-last-layer-pll/), [Park example](https://ruwix.com/blog/max-park-rubik-single-record-313/)  
A final top-face alignment may remain; that shortcut belongs to this solution, not automatically to everyone facing the scramble. [Park reconstruction](https://reco.nz/solve/9155), [study distinction](../METHOD.md)
